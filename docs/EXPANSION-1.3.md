# Expansion review · v1.3.0

12 October 2026 · 16 added species · 32 new SVGs · 68 species / 136 SVGs in total

## Selection and sequence

This release extends v1.2.0 in two rounds of eight species. The first round covers further European forest, riparian and wild-fruit species: Field elm, Grey alder, Common whitebeam, European crab apple, Wild pear, Wayfaring tree, White poplar and Service tree. It adds the third European elm and additional alder, poplar, Sorbus and Viburnum forms.

The first eight were compared with botanical descriptions, drawn in both sizes, corrected after enlarged inspection, checked at 24/32 px Compact and 48/64 px Detail, and passed the automated geometry checks before work began on the second group.

The second round adds Ginkgo, London plane, Tulip tree, American sweetgum, Japanese maple, Italian maple, Common ivy and Turkish hazel. It extends familiar planted trees, adds two distinct maple forms and introduces a woody climber. These additions followed the same source, drawing, visual review and geometry sequence. The choices represent species found in Central Europe, not exclusively native species or a planting recommendation.

## Morphology decisions

The table in [BOTANY.md](BOTANY.md) links every species and specifies what each size retains or omits. The following sources resolve the main drawing decisions; descriptions are paraphrased, and no reference photograph is traced or redistributed.

### Round one

- **Field elm:** [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/ulmus-minor.html) gives 8–14 lateral nerves per side. [Fragnière, Küffer & Kozlowski (2022), table 1](https://www.infoflora.ch/assets/content/documents/publication/Y_Fragniere_2022_Bestimmung_europaeischen_Ulmen.pdf) supports a single apex, strong basal asymmetry, maximum width near the middle, 5–15 mm petioles and upper nerve branching. Detail uses twelve main pairs and four upper branches. Compact uses six pairs and omits the forks. This preserves the earlier Wych and European white elm drawings; it does not force all elm leaves into three disjoint silhouettes.
- **Grey alder:** [Flora Helvetica via Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/alnus-incana.html) describes an ovate, pointed blade widest below the middle and usually 10–15 vein pairs, occasionally eight. The drawing selects twelve pairs in Detail and six in Compact. Its pointed apex and lower maximum width contrast with the existing blunt, obovate Black alder.
- **Common whitebeam:** [FloraWeb, BfN](https://www.floraweb.de/php/artenhome.php?name-use-id=5697) gives an ovate to elliptic blade and 10–14 vein pairs; [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/sorbus-aria.html) describes double toothing and a white-felted underside. The drawing selects an unlobed form with twelve pairs; Compact retains six. Neither variant attempts to encode the felt using invented edge marks.
- **European crab apple:** [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/malus-sylvestris.html) describes broad ovate, finely toothed leaves and a petiole usually only slightly shorter than the blade. Both variants have a conspicuously long stalk. The distinction from cultivated apples also involves characters that are absent here; the outline does not establish genetic purity or wild origin.
- **Wild pear:** [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/pyrus-pyraster.html) permits finely toothed or entire blades and petioles up to the blade length. This icon selects a rounded, finely toothed form with a short apical point and a long stalk. Its shape is a selected example, not a universally reliable apple/pear discriminator.
- **Wayfaring tree:** [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/viburnum-lantana.html) describes broad oval to ovate leaves, regular fine teeth, a wrinkled surface and stellate felt below. Both drawings preserve the unlobed outline and fine teeth; surface relief, felt and opposite insertion are absent. The seven/four drawn vein pairs are optical choices.
- **White poplar:** [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/populus-alba.html) distinguishes rounded-ovate, irregularly toothed or lobed foliage from vigorous sprout leaves with 3–9 palmate lobes. The icon deliberately selects a five-lobed vigorous-shoot leaf. Many short-shoot leaves look different. The white underside and petiole cross-section are not represented.
- **Service tree:** the [European Atlas of Forest Tree Species, Enescu et al. (2016)](https://ies-ows.jrc.ec.europa.eu/efdac/download/Atlas/pdf/Sorbus_domestica.pdf) gives 13–21 leaflets. This drawing uses thirteen. [Flora Helvetica via Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/sorbus-domestica.html) specifies an untoothed lower third of each leaflet and 12–15 secondary vein pairs per leaflet. Both sizes preserve the basal entire margin; that dense secondary venation is omitted in both, rather than replaced with a misleading small count. Detail shows leaflet midribs; Compact omits them.

### Round two

- **Ginkgo:** [Flora of North America](https://www.efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=200005235) describes a fan-shaped blade, commonly wider than long, a variable apical notch, dichotomous venation and a long petiole. The FNA description was available through indexed text and a [University of Hamburg-hosted transcription](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/b-online/earle/gi/gi/index.htm); the direct eFloras page did not open reliably during this review. The drawing has two successive bifurcations in Detail and one in Compact, with no midrib. Natural vein density is greatly reduced. Ginkgo is a gymnosperm; the catalog’s `broadleaf` group is explicitly based on blade form, not membership of flowering plants.
- **London plane:** [Flora Helvetica via Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/platanus-%C3%97hispanica.html) describes the hybrid as less deeply divided than Oriental plane, with a truncate to cordate base and a central lobe about as wide as, or wider than, long. This selected five-lobed form has coarse teeth and a broad central lobe. The name follows that flora: _Platanus × hispanica_; _P. × acerifolia_ is also widely used. Natural hybrid variation and alternate insertion are not encoded.
- **Tulip tree:** [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/liriodendron-tulipifera.html) describes four lobes, a shallowly notched apex, a conspicuous midrib, pinnate venation and long petioles. The drawing keeps four lateral primary nerves at two levels, rather than a maple-like palmate hub. Compact removes minor branches but retains the apical notch and entire margin.
- **American sweetgum:** [TH OWL, Botanical Garden Höxter](https://botanischer-garten-hoexter.de/pflanze-liquidambar_styraciflua.html) describes long-stalked, serrate leaves with five, rarely seven, lobes and alternate insertion. The selected five-lobed star has fine teeth and pointed lobes. Alternate insertion would require a shoot illustration and is not shown; the leaf can resemble some maples.
- **Japanese maple:** [Flora of China 11, Acer treatment, species 20](https://www.iplant.cn/foc/pdf/Acer.pdf) gives five or seven lobes extending to about mid-blade, lanceolate lobes, long-acuminate tips, irregular double serration and petioles 2–6 cm for blades 3–6 cm long. The selected seven-lobed form is a simple blade, not seven leaflets or a deeply dissected cultivar. Compact reduces the teeth; both keep the seven main veins.
- **Italian maple / Schneeballblättriger Ahorn:** [Flora Helvetica via Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/acer-opalus.html) describes three or five lobes restricted approximately to the outer third, with blunt to slightly pointed tips and several wavy teeth. The selected broad five-lobed form has much shallower divisions than Japanese maple and fewer, softer teeth than Norway maple.
- **Common ivy:** [Flora Helvetica via Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/hedera-helix.html) separates angular, three- to five-lobed foliage on nonflowering shoots from unlobed oval to rhombic leaves on flowering shoots. This icon selects a five-lobed juvenile climbing leaf with an entire margin. It does not depict the adult flowering form.
- **Turkish hazel:** [Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/corylus-colurna.html) describes ovate-cordate pointed blades, double teeth, 1.5–3 cm petioles and five or six vein pairs. Detail has six pairs, Compact three. Neither shows the vein hairs or fruit involucre; resemblance to Common hazel is expected.

## Corrections during review

- Field elm’s unequal base, upper nerve forks and the spacing of the basal veins were revised. Grey alder and whitebeam vein positions were adjusted to avoid dense crossbars near the blade base.
- Service tree leaflets were widened, shortened and respaced after the first enlarged rendering. Both sizes retain separate painted leaflet outlines. The entire lower margin is built into the contour.
- Two minor White poplar veins were removed where they gave an unconvincing attachment. The five main palmate directions remain.
- Sweetgum’s lower lobes were extended, and the basal contours of sweetgum and Japanese maple were broadened. Compact vein endpoints were moved to leave more visible space near the lower outlines.
- Long names in the overview now wrap inside their cell, including Schneeballblättriger Ahorn. The gallery description was updated to include all three poplars.

## Final review and limits

Both optical sizes of all sixteen additions were inspected enlarged, then on light and dark backgrounds at 24/32 px Compact and 48/64 px Detail. Comparison sheets grouped all three European elms, the two alders, the two hazels, apple and pear, rowan and service tree, all three poplars, the two viburnums and related lobed leaves.

The full 136-file collection is subject to the tests recorded in [QUALITY.md](QUALITY.md), including complete-stroke containment, contour separation and actual Chromium/Firefox workflows. A SHA-256 comparison against the canonical v1.2.0 archive verifies all 104 existing source SVGs unchanged. The preserved set includes the earlier oak, elm, poplar and conifer revisions.

This is a source-guided design review, not an independent botanical certification. Vein and tooth counts, margin microstructure, surface hairs, leaf arrangement and three-dimensional anatomy cannot all survive reduction to a 24 px pictogram. Fine serration may not remain visible at small sizes. Closely related species retain natural similarities. Use labels alongside the icons and consult a botanical key for identification.
