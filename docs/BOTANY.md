# Botanical notes

Version 1.0.0 · 5 October 2026

The drawings preserve characteristic blade outlines, leaf arrangements, lobes and relative petiole lengths. They show selected typical forms, with each species scaled independently. Botanical descriptions guide the artwork; these icons are not a complete identification key or independently certified botanical plates.

## Conifer construction

**Norway spruce:** the needle arrangement is a simplified projection of spiral insertion around a twig. Individual needles have narrow bodies and pointed ends; varied projected lengths and directions indicate different positions around the axis. Detail contains 31 visible needles, Compact 17. Short woody attachment pegs are indicated on selected lateral needles in Detail. The projection is constructed, not a measurement of one specimen or an assertion of an exact universal divergence angle. Needles near the line of sight and the shoot’s full natural density are reduced for legibility. Overlaps between separate projected needles are intentional; self-crossing needle outlines are not.

This follows the [Hohenheim botanical garden description](https://virtuelle-gaerten.uni-hohenheim.de/4DAction/W_Init/HG_Unit_de?UnitPK=HOH-DS-1-30420) and [Gregor Aas, LWF Wissen 80](https://lwf.bayern.de/boden-klima/baumartenwahl/171646/index.php): individual spiral needles, pointed tips and persistent woody attachment pegs. Side shoots can appear parted below; a flat arrangement is therefore possible in nature, but is not the selected form here. The four-sided needle cross-section cannot be established from this silhouette.

**Scots pine:** the drawing isolates three short shoots on a twig. Each has exactly two pointed needles and one shared basal sheath in both variants. The sheath is its own closed SVG path (leaf-sheath), not a third needle or an arbitrary crossbar. Needle bases overlap the sheath and every sheath meets the twig. Gently curved outlines suggest the overall needle form; they do not claim to depict axial torsion or cross-sectional anatomy.

The [University of Göttingen account](https://www.uni-goettingen.de/de/36744.html) describes stiff, twisted needles in pairs within a sheath. [Gregor Aas, LWF Wissen 57](https://lwf.bayern.de/boden-klima/baumartenwahl/024782/index.php) explains the long-shoot/short-shoot organization of pines. Two-needle fascicles alone do not distinguish Scots pine from every other pine species.

**Silver fir:** the selected shade shoot has flat-looking needles with nearly parallel margins beyond their narrowed base. Detail includes a shallow terminal notch; Compact retains a blunt tip. The notched tip follows the linked Flora Helvetica account. The underside and its two pale stomatal bands are not shown.

All seven conifer species are checked at 16× resolution for unintended detached needles or twig segments. Every current conifer drawing forms one connected painted component. This catches attachment gaps, not biological identity.

## Oak silhouettes

The oak drawings use an elongated blade, staggered rounded lobes and a gently curved midrib. Pedunculate oak is broader above the middle, with small basal auricles and a very short petiole. Sessile oak has shallower sinuses, a tapering base without auricles and a longer petiole. These are selected typical forms; natural leaves vary and the species can hybridize.

Detail pedunculate oak includes two intercalary veins directed toward sinuses. Sessile oak veins run toward lobes. This distinction follows the [Bavarian State Institute of Forestry comparison](https://www.waldwissen.net/de/lebensraum-wald/baeume-und-waldpflanzen/laubbaeume/trauben-oder-stieleiche) and the species descriptions above. Compact omits the intercalary veins and reduces the number of lateral veins for legibility.

## Wych elm construction

The drawing coordinates each lateral vein with a specific marginal tooth. The blade is elongated and widest above its midpoint, with a softly curved, mildly unequal base and a very short petiole. Its outline spans about 77.0 × 43.8 viewBox units (length:width ≈ 1.76); the petiole is about 3.6 units long, approximately 4.7% of blade length. These are construction measurements, not universal species ratios.

Detail contains 13 main lateral veins per side and six branches in the upper half. The veins curve obliquely toward the apex and end at tooth tips. Branches begin on their parent veins and end at separate teeth. The narrowed central apex has two restrained lateral points and additional small teeth. Compact preserves the overall outline and short petiole while merging the secondary teeth and retaining five main lateral veins per side. Its stronger strokes are adjusted separately for small sizes.

[Fragnière, Küffer & Kozlowski (2022), table 1 and figures 2a, 2a* and 2a**](https://www.infoflora.ch/assets/content/documents/publication/Y_Fragniere_2022_Bestimmung_europaeischen_Ulmen.pdf) support the upper maximum width, only slight basal asymmetry, 2–7 mm petiole, double teeth and upper vein branching. Their specimen photographs also guided the relationship between veins and marginal teeth. [Flora Helvetica via Info Flora](https://www.infoflora.ch/de/flora/ulmus-glabra.html) gives 12–20 lateral veins per side; 13 is the selected drawing count. The [University of Göttingen account](https://www.uni-goettingen.de/de/56392.html) provides an additional comparison for blade proportions, the very short petiole and possible three-pointed apex.

Lateral apex points vary among leaves; a single pointed leaf can also be normal for this species. The chosen form is a constructed synthesis of documented features, not a traced specimen or a complete diagnosis.

## Deliberate reductions

- Compact reduces veins, teeth and minor notches to remain readable at 24–40 px. Detail is intended for 48 px and above, preferably at least 64 px for fine veins.
- Larch shows 11 needles per bunch in Detail and 7 in Compact; natural bunches contain many more. The two separated fans are a flat schematic view.
- Silver fir shows a relatively flat, comb-like shade shoot. Detail retains the tip notch; Compact omits it. Spruce retains front/side needle projections in both sizes, with independently reduced counts and optically adjusted widths.
- Needle cross-sections, hairs, stomatal bands, undersides and the lateral flattening of an aspen petiole cannot be fully shown in this monochrome outline system.
- Aspen uses a rounded short-shoot leaf. Vigorous long-shoot leaves can look different; the petiole is about blade length, not universally longer.
- Compound-leaf counts are representative: ash 9, rowan 11, black locust 9 and horse chestnut 7. Leaflets are spaced apart for small-size readability.
- White willow remains lanceolate. Its blade is broadened optically and Compact uses a smooth margin; fine teeth are retained in Detail.
- Maple contours and teeth are reduced more strongly in Compact. Field maple selects a five-lobed form; three-lobed forms also occur.
- Wych elm selects a three-pointed leaf form: lateral apex points occur naturally but are not present on every leaf. Detail has 13 main lateral veins on each side and six upper branches; Compact has five main veins on each side and no branches. All drawn lateral veins and branches meet actual marginal teeth. Compact merges the fine secondary teeth. Neither variant depicts surface hairs or underside characters.
- Hornbeam has twelve vein pairs in Detail and six in Compact. Sweet chestnut has twelve and six drawn pairs respectively, each running to a major marginal tooth.
- Proportions describe typical forms, not measurements of one specimen. Age, shoot type, sun/shade and natural variation affect real leaves. Fine features should not be used diagnostically at 24 px.

## Drawing checks

All 56 SVGs are checked for valid XML, metadata, labels, licensing, rendering and viewport bounds. Blade, filled-needle and sheath contours are tested for self-crossings. Separate leaflet blades are also checked for collisions, including outline width. Conifer drawings are tested for disconnected painted parts. All 341 internal vein/midrib paths pass containment and full-stroke raster checks. These checks establish drawing properties; they do not prove botanical correctness. The [quality record](QUALITY.md) documents the visual review, browser results and numerical tolerances.
